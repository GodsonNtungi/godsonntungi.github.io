Concept 1

One window is a 15 × 25 picture of loudness

Each test row is 15 minutes of underwater sound. For every minute we get the loudness of 25 pitch ranges, from a low 32 Hz rumble to a high 7.9 kHz hiss. Draw it as a grid: time runs left to right, pitch runs bottom to top, and brighter means louder.

This one has a loud added ship. You can see the bright band in the low pitches (bottom) where the ship rumbles. That's all the information we get: no actual sound, just these 375 numbers.

Physics used: sound weakens with distance (twice as far is 6 dB quieter), and high pitches also fade faster over distance, so far ships sound deeper. Ship size is how loud the ship is 1 km away, compared to the sea.

Concept 2

Sounds add up as energy, not as decibels

Loudness is measured in decibels, which are squashed: every +10 dB means 10 times the energy. So a ship 10 dB quieter than the sea is only about 9% of the total, and a ship as loud as the sea is 50%. Drag the sliders.

Concept 3

Five kinds of window

Every window falls into one of these. The tricky one is the second: a ship was already in the recording. It sounds like a ship, but it is part of the background and does not count.
